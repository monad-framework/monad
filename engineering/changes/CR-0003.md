# CR-0003 — Establish Monad AI-Driven Engineering Operating Model

**State:** PROPOSED
**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Authority required:** Project Steward / Architecture Owner, with affected Product, Engineering, Security, and Operations authority  
**Change class:** Architecture / requirements / EOS lifecycle / governance / specification baseline

## Summary

Establish a Monad-native AI-Driven Engineering Operating Model that makes AI proactive in engineering workflow analysis, planning, clarification, artifact generation, and bounded execution while preserving EOS as the sole lifecycle/control plane, human-sovereign authority for consequential decisions, deterministic policy gates, evidence-based acceptance, and one canonical operational state.

The target operating principle is:

> **AI drives the work. EOS governs the work. Humans govern consequential meaning and authority. Evidence governs acceptance.**

The model is architectural composition rather than adoption of an external development lifecycle. It extends the selective-assimilation direction accepted by ADR-0006 and CR-0001 and the post-MVP product direction accepted by CR-0002 without reopening the active MVP implementation path.

## Decision pressure

Monad already contains the principal primitives required for governed AI-driven engineering: canonical engineering knowledge, explicit authority levels, provenance, typed trace relationships, Work Packets, dependency-aware planning, policy gates, bounded execution, verification evidence, review, change control, and a single canonical EOS operational state.

The current product requirements also commit Monad to bounded agent context, unified memory/intelligence/execution, anti-hallucination memory, dependency-aware agent work, progressive autonomy, cross-harness review, provider-neutral AI routing, declarative governance/audit, and execution observability.

What is not yet explicit is the cross-layer operating model that determines how those capabilities work together. In particular, Monad requires a normative answer to these questions:

1. May AI proactively initiate engineering analysis and workflow progression rather than act only as a passive assistant?
2. How does AI initiative remain distinct from engineering authority?
3. How are adaptive engineering pathways derived without creating a second lifecycle?
4. When must ambiguity become a governed decision instead of an AI assumption?
5. How do planning, approval, execution, verification, and review remain reconstructably traceable across different AI providers and harnesses?
6. How does higher autonomy remain bounded, evidence-backed, observable, and revocable?
7. How do operations and verification evidence feed future AI reasoning without becoming canonical truth automatically?

Without an explicit operating model, these concerns risk being implemented independently and inconsistently across EOSP, EOSE, EOSV, EOSR, policy, agent contracts, observability, and later orchestration work.

## Proposed operating model

Formally:

```text
Monad AI-Driven Engineering Operating Model
=
Monad Semantic Authority
+ EOS Governance
+ AI-Driven Workflow Initiative
+ Bounded AI Execution
+ Human Sovereignty
+ Evidence-Based Closure
```

Fundamental distinctions:

```text
AI initiative != AI authority
AI output != canonical engineering truth
Human sovereignty != mandatory human micromanagement
```

The target governed loop is:

```text
INTENT
  -> CONTEXT COMPILATION
  -> PLAN
  -> CLARIFY
  -> DECIDE
  -> AUTHORIZE
  -> EXECUTE
  -> VERIFY
  -> REVIEW
  -> INTEGRATE / RELEASE / OPERATE
  -> OBSERVE
  -> LEARN
  -> REPLAN
```

The inner AI-driven loop is:

```text
PROPOSE -> CLARIFY -> DECIDE -> APPROVE -> EXECUTE -> VERIFY
```

This loop operates inside EOS. It MUST NOT become a ninth EOS lifecycle layer or a parallel control plane.

## Normative invariants to establish

The design SHALL establish requirements equivalent to the following invariants:

1. **Intent traceability.** Every governed execution traces to authorized engineering intent.
2. **Initiative without authority.** AI may initiate analysis, planning, clarification, recommendations, and proposed transitions without obtaining approval authority merely by doing so.
3. **Ambiguity escalation.** Material ambiguity is surfaced rather than silently resolved for consequential product, architecture, security, governance, legal, operational, or irreversible decisions.
4. **Adaptive workflow.** Engineering breadth and depth adapt to risk, complexity, lifecycle state, dependencies, evidence, and uncertainty.
5. **Explainable rigor.** Materially skipped, reordered, added, or deepened activities are explainable.
6. **Native decision semantics.** Consequential decisions use existing Monad authority, Decision, Approval, ADR, Change Request, Review, and related native semantics.
7. **No probabilistic promotion.** AI output does not become normative because of confidence, repetition, or model identity.
8. **Bounded execution.** Execution remains inside authorized Work Packet, scope, environment, capability, and autonomy boundaries.
9. **Governing drift.** Material governing-input drift invalidates or suspends stale execution authority.
10. **Evidence independence.** Executor assertions and self-tests are not sufficient proof where independent verification is required.
11. **Independent review.** Review independence is enforceable according to policy, consequence, and risk.
12. **Reconstructability.** Consequential planning, decisions, approvals, execution, verification, review, changes, and acceptance are reconstructably traceable.
13. **Transcript non-authority.** Raw AI transcripts are not mandatory canonical engineering state.
14. **Provider neutrality.** AI provider, model, harness, or vendor identity does not become semantic authority.
15. **Progressive autonomy.** Higher autonomy is explicit, evidence-backed, bounded, observable, policy-controlled, and revocable.
16. **Evidence feedback.** Engineering and operational evidence may inform later reasoning but retains its actual authority classification.

## Canonical-model constraints

This change MUST NOT introduce a parallel AI state model.

`.eos/state/current.json` remains the sole current EOS operational authority under the canonical-state model.

The design MUST reuse existing Monad concepts before introducing new canonical entity families. In particular, plans, decisions, approvals, dependencies, reviews, evidence, traceability, Work Packets, Work Cycles, Program Increments, and Change Requests should continue to use existing native concepts such as `DEC`, `APR`, `DEP`, `REV`, `EVID`, `TRC`, `WP`, `WC`, `PI`, and `CR` where those semantics are sufficient.

The change MUST NOT create canonical families merely because AI is involved, including parallel `AI-PLAN`, `AI-TASK`, `AI-DECISION`, `AI-APPROVAL`, `AI-EVIDENCE`, `AI-WORKFLOW`, or `AI-CONTEXT` stores without a separately demonstrated semantic gap and governed decision.

## Autonomy model

AI-assisted, AI-driven, and bounded AI-autonomous work SHALL be treated as profiles within one Monad operating model rather than competing lifecycle methodologies.

Autonomy controls binding action and delegated capability. It does not determine whether an AI may inspect, reason, identify ambiguity, generate options, or make recommendations.

Greater analytical capability does not automatically confer greater authority.

Human-sovereign authority remains grounded in accountability, governance, delegated authority, risk ownership, mission, and irreversible consequences rather than an assumption that humans will always possess superior analytical ability.

## Relationship to external AI-driven development models

External AI-driven development frameworks are design donors and compatibility targets only.

Useful concepts MAY be assimilated, including:

- AI-initiated workflow analysis;
- AI-generated plans;
- proactive clarification;
- adaptive workflow breadth and depth;
- human disposition of consequential decisions;
- bounded execution after authorization;
- persistent governed context;
- end-to-end traceability;
- explicit verification and review.

External lifecycle names, task hierarchies, dependency graphs, approval stores, evidence stores, canonical state, or vendor-specific steering mechanisms MUST NOT become competing Monad authority.

## Required conformance scenarios

The normative tranche must define or trace acceptance behavior for at least:

1. a small low-risk reversible change receiving a lightweight pathway;
2. ambiguous product intent requiring an explicit decision instead of AI guessing;
3. architecture change discovered during execution routing through EOSC;
4. bounded autonomous execution of an explicitly authorized low-risk Work Packet;
5. security-sensitive work increasing required rigor and authority;
6. independent review that cannot be satisfied by trivial executor self-review;
7. governing-input drift invalidating a stale execution contract;
8. AI provider failure without corruption of canonical engineering state;
9. an authorized human denial causing replanning rather than repeated pressure for the rejected outcome;
10. verification evidence invalidating an assumption and routing to replanning/change control.

## Alternatives

### Alternative A — Keep AI as an informal assistant convention

Continue using AI through prompts and executor-specific workflows without a cross-layer operating model.

**Rejected direction.** This leaves initiative, authority, clarification, adaptive planning, autonomy, review independence, and evidence feedback underspecified and likely to diverge across implementations.

### Alternative B — Adopt an external AI-DLC/AI-SDLC lifecycle directly

Install or model an external lifecycle alongside EOS.

**Rejected.** This conflicts with ADR-0006 and risks duplicate lifecycle state, task hierarchy, authority, evidence, and orchestration semantics.

### Alternative C — Treat fully autonomous development as the target lifecycle

Permit sufficiently capable AI to plan, decide, execute, verify, and accept work as one autonomous authority.

**Rejected.** Analytical capability is not equivalent to delegated authority or accountable risk ownership. This also collapses separation of execution, verification, review, and acceptance.

### Alternative D — Monad-native AI-driven, human-governed, evidence-closed operating model

Allow AI to drive analysis and authorized engineering activity while EOS controls lifecycle transitions, native authority controls consequential decisions, and evidence/review control acceptance.

**Recommended.** This composes existing Monad capabilities without creating a competing source of truth.

## Risks

1. **Over-governance.** Adaptive planning could become ceremony rather than reducing it. Mitigation: require lightweight pathways for low-risk reversible work and explainable depth escalation.
2. **Authority ambiguity.** Proactive AI behavior could be mistaken for approval. Mitigation: make recommendation, decision, approval, authorization, execution, verification, and acceptance distinct states/records.
3. **Autonomy escalation.** Implementation may grant broader capability before evidence supports it. Mitigation: progressive trust, explicit capability grants, revocation, independent verification, and risk-class policy.
4. **Duplicate semantics.** New AI-specific objects may reproduce existing DEC/APR/DEP/EVID/REV/TRC/WP concepts. Mitigation: reuse native entities by default and require a separate semantic-gap justification for additions.
5. **Provider coupling.** A convenient executor may leak provider-specific concepts into architecture. Mitigation: provider-neutral contracts with adapters.
6. **Audit overload or sensitive transcripts.** Capturing every model interaction could create excessive or sensitive records. Mitigation: preserve material structured decisions/evidence/provenance while keeping raw transcript retention configurable and non-canonical.
7. **MVP disruption.** A broad EOS program could interrupt the semantic-kernel critical path. Mitigation: keep the tranche parallel and separately planned; do not broaden active MVP packets.
8. **Policy complexity.** Too many dynamic gates could reduce determinism. Mitigation: retain constrained deterministic predicates and explicit authority classes.

## Validation required before implementation planning

Before implementation Work Packets are authorized, the normative tranche should demonstrate that:

- ADR-0006 remains intact and authoritative;
- no second lifecycle or current-state authority has been introduced;

- requirement/specification traceability is complete;
- executor/reviewer independence can be expressed without provider lock-in;
