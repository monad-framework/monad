# Governance

Governance defines who may decide, which evidence is required, how changes are
controlled, and how project records remain trustworthy. Its purpose is faster,
clearer accountable decisions—not bureaucracy for routine reversible work.

## Governance principles

- Authority is explicit, scoped, and paired with accountability.
- Decisions are made at the lowest level with sufficient context and authority.
- Consequence, irreversibility, and uncertainty determine review depth.
- Evidence and dissent are recorded for material decisions.
- Emergency authority is narrow and followed by retrospective review.
- Conflicts of interest are disclosed and managed.
- No document or process overrides law, safety obligations, or ethical duties.

## Precedence

When approved sources conflict, use this order: legal and ethical obligations;
governance and accepted decisions; vision; product baseline; architecture;
specifications; engineering plans; explanatory documentation. The higher-level
source does not answer lower-level implementation questions, but it constrains
acceptable answers.

## Governance review

Review governance after a material incident, repeated decision delay, recurring
waiver, organizational change, or at least annually. Simplify controls that do
not improve decisions or evidence. Strengthen controls where consequence or
observed failure justifies them.
