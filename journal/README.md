# Project Journal

The journal preserves a chronological narrative of discovery, decisions,
turning points, failures, and lessons. It explains how the project evolved
without replacing normative requirements, ADRs, specifications, or status
records.

## Entry format

Name entries `NNNN-short-kebab-title.md` using sequential immutable numbers.
Each entry contains date, author or role, context, observations, decisions or
questions, links to authoritative artifacts, and what changed as a result.

## What belongs here

- why the project began or changed direction;
- synthesis across multiple decisions or work cycles;
- important surprises and invalidated assumptions;
- lessons from experiments, milestones, releases, or incidents;
- a durable narrative useful to future contributors.

Routine status, meeting minutes without consequence, raw research data, and
normative policy belong in their dedicated sources. A journal entry may quote
or summarize them but links to the authoritative record.

## Integrity

Published entries are not rewritten to make the past appear more certain.
Correct factual errors transparently and add later interpretation in a new
entry or labeled amendment. Never include secrets, private participant details,
security exploit material, or sensitive incident evidence.

## Cadence

Write at project inception and after material milestones, pivots, rejected
hypotheses, architecture commitments, releases, and incident lessons. Quality
matters more than calendar frequency.
