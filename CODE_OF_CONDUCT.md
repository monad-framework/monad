# Community Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to a productive, inclusive, and harassment-free community for
people of every background and level of experience. Participation is a choice;
respectful conduct is a condition of participation.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and evidence without attacking people.
- Assume good intent initially, then respond to demonstrated behavior.
- Give specific, actionable feedback and accept correction professionally.
- Respect names, pronouns, privacy, boundaries, and differing communication
  needs.
- Credit others' work and disclose conflicts of interest.
- Protect confidential, personal, and security-sensitive information.
- Make space for quieter participants and avoid dominating decisions.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized attention, deliberate
misgendering, stalking, doxxing, sustained disruption, retaliation, and
publication of private information are prohibited. Technical disagreement,
firm moderation, and good-faith critique are not harassment when expressed
without abuse.

## Scope

This policy applies in project repositories, reviews, issue trackers, events,
meetings, chat spaces, and public interactions where a person represents the
project. Private conduct may be considered when it creates a credible risk to
community safety.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to thomasjcarter613@gmail.com with the subject
`Conduct report`. Include the location, approximate time, people involved,
available evidence, immediate safety concerns, and the outcome you seek. Avoid
public escalation when it could expose a target or intensify harm.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will limit disclosure to those needed for a fair review. Depending
on severity and pattern, responses may include clarification, a private
warning, required corrective action, temporary participation limits, removal
from project spaces, or permanent exclusion. Immediate restrictions may be
used to protect people while facts are reviewed.

Anyone handling a report must recuse themselves when personally involved or
unable to act impartially. Retaliation against reporters, witnesses, or those
assisting an investigation is itself a serious violation.

## Appeals

A sanctioned participant may submit one written appeal identifying a material
factual or procedural error. A maintainer not involved in the original action
will review the appeal when possible. Disagreement with a documented rule is
not by itself grounds for reversal.
