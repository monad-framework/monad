# ADR-0008: AI-Driven Engineering as the Default EOS Operating Model

* **Status:** Accepted
* **Date:** 2026-09-05
* **Decision owners:** Architecture Owner, Engineering Owner
* **Reviewers:** Project Steward, Product Owner, Security Owner, Operations Owner, Verification Owner as affected
* **Related:** ADR-0006, ADR-0007, CR-0003, `governance/authority.md`, `governance/canonical-state-model.md`, `governance/decision-process.md`, `governance/planning-engine.md`, `governance/policy-engine.md`, `governance/execution-engine.md`, `engineering/lifecycle/README.md`, `engineering/definition-of-ready.md`, `product/product-requirements.md`
* **Supersedes:** none
* **Superseded by:** none
* **Acceptance evidence:** `engineering/reviews/DECISION-0003-2026-09-05-adr-0008-acceptance.md`

## Decision summary

Monad SHALL establish **AI-driven, human-governed, evidence-closed engineering** as the default operating model for engineering work where suitable AI capability is available.

The governing principle is:

> **AI drives the work. EOS governs the work. Humans govern consequential meaning and authority. Evidence governs acceptance.**

AI MAY proactively analyze intent, compile governed context, propose engineering pathways, identify ambiguity, generate candidate artifacts, recommend lifecycle progression, perform bounded authorized execution, interpret verification results, identify changed assumptions, and propose replanning.

AI initiative SHALL NOT itself confer engineering authority.

EOS SHALL remain the sole repository lifecycle and engineering control plane. Canonical Monad artifacts, canonical EOS operational state, explicit authority, deterministic policy, governed decisions, approvals, verification evidence, review, and change control remain authoritative according to their existing semantics.

AI output SHALL NOT become canonical engineering truth merely because it was generated by an AI system, repeated by multiple models, assigned high confidence, or produced by a preferred provider.

Human sovereignty SHALL mean accountable control over consequential authority, delegation, mission, accepted risk, irreversible commitments, and escalation. It SHALL NOT require humans to manually perform or approve every low-risk engineering action.

The operating model SHALL support multiple autonomy profiles—including AI-assisted, AI-driven, and bounded AI-autonomous execution—within one EOS-governed model rather than as competing development methodologies.

This ADR extends and applies ADR-0006. It does not supersede ADR-0006.

ADR-0007, if accepted, provides the governed execution boundary through which AI and other executors perform authorized effects. ADR-0008 does not depend on ADR-0007 becoming Accepted in order to define the broader operating model, but the two decisions are designed to compose.

## Context

Monad has evolved beyond a documentation system or task orchestrator into an Engineering Knowledge Compilation Platform with a repository-native Engineering Operating System.

Its current architecture already contains the principal mechanisms required for governed AI-driven engineering:

* canonical engineering artifacts;
* semantic relationships and traceability;
* explicit authority;
* provenance;
* decisions and approvals;
* Program Increments, Work Cycles, and Work Packets;
* dependency-aware planning;
* deterministic policy gates;
* bounded execution;
* verification evidence;
* independent review;
* change control;
* release lifecycle;
* maintenance;
* one canonical EOS operational state.

ADR-0006 established that useful concepts from external AI-oriented software-development systems may be selectively assimilated into Monad-native semantics while EOS remains the sole lifecycle/control plane.

The repository also contains a proposed ADR-0007 defining a Governed Execution Harness as the boundary between EOS authority and replaceable executors.

What remains undefined is the **cross-layer operating model** governing how AI participates throughout engineering rather than only during execution.

Without such a decision, individual capabilities risk emerging independently:

* AI planning may develop without a shared authority model;
* context compilation may become provider-specific;
* clarification may remain an informal prompting behavior;
* autonomy may be interpreted differently across execution systems;
* AI-generated recommendations may be confused with approval;
* workflow adaptation may accidentally become a competing lifecycle;
* evidence feedback may be mistaken for canonical truth;
* review independence may depend on harness conventions;
* agent orchestration may grow before its authority boundaries are explicit.

An explicit architectural decision is therefore required before Monad expands further into adaptive planning, AI-driven workflow initiative, progressive autonomy, cross-harness execution, multi-agent orchestration, or AI-mediated operations.

## Decision drivers

1. **Single control plane.** EOS must remain the sole lifecycle and operational engineering control plane.

2. **AI initiative.** AI should be able to proactively advance analysis and propose work rather than remain a passive autocomplete mechanism.

3. **Authority integrity.** Initiative, recommendation, execution capability, approval, and acceptance must remain distinct.

4. **Human sovereignty.** Consequential authority must remain accountable and explicitly delegated.

5. **Adaptive rigor.** Engineering process depth should scale with risk, complexity, uncertainty, reversibility, dependencies, and evidence.

6. **Determinism before probability.** Structural facts, policy predicates, lifecycle state, authority, and other deterministic constraints should precede probabilistic judgment.

7. **Evidence-based closure.** Completion must depend on evidence and governed acceptance rather than executor self-report.

8. **Reconstructability.** Consequential engineering activity must remain attributable and reconstructable.

9. **Provider neutrality.** Models, vendors, agent harnesses, and execution environments must remain replaceable.

10. **Progressive autonomy.** Higher autonomy must be earned, bounded, inspectable, and revocable.

11. **Semantic reuse.** Existing Monad concepts should be reused before introducing AI-specific entity families.

12. **Local-first operation.** The model must not require a mandatory external AI control plane.

13. **Failure containment.** Model failure, provider failure, hallucination, or contradictory AI output must not corrupt canonical engineering state.

14. **MVP protection.** This operating-model decision must not silently broaden currently authorized product Work Packets.

## Fundamental distinctions

The architecture SHALL preserve the following distinctions:

```text
AI initiative != AI authority

AI output != canonical engineering truth

AI recommendation != decision

Decision != approval

Readiness != authorization

Execution != verification

Verification != review

Review != acceptance

Analytical capability != delegated authority

Human sovereignty != mandatory human micromanagement
```

These distinctions apply regardless of whether the executor is a single model, an agent harness, a multi-agent system, a human engineer, a script, or a future non-human intelligence.

## Target operating model

The Monad AI-Driven Engineering Operating Model is:

```text
Monad Semantic Authority
+ EOS Governance
+ AI-Driven Workflow Initiative
+ Bounded Execution
+ Human Sovereignty
+ Evidence-Based Closure
```

The full engineering loop is:

```text
INTENT
  ↓
CONTEXT COMPILATION
  ↓
PLAN
  ↓
CLARIFY
  ↓
DECIDE
  ↓
AUTHORIZE
  ↓
EXECUTE
  ↓
VERIFY
  ↓
REVIEW
  ↓
INTEGRATE / RELEASE / OPERATE
  ↓
OBSERVE
  ↓
LEARN
  ↓
REPLAN
```

A recurring inner loop is:

```text
PROPOSE
  → CLARIFY
  → DECIDE
  → APPROVE
  → EXECUTE
  → VERIFY
```

These are operating-model activities, not a new lifecycle state machine.

They SHALL operate through existing EOS lifecycle layers and native Monad concepts.

The model MUST NOT become a ninth EOS layer.

## Options considered

### Option A — AI-assisted engineering as the default model

AI primarily responds to human requests, generates candidate artifacts, and assists with implementation. Humans remain responsible for initiating most workflow progression.

#### Advantages

* simple mental model;
* low autonomy risk;
* minimal new governance requirements;
* familiar to current development teams.

#### Disadvantages

* underuses AI planning and coordination capability;
* requires humans to remain the workflow scheduler;
* creates unnecessary interaction overhead;
* makes proactive ambiguity discovery inconsistent;
* does not provide a coherent path to higher autonomy;
* encourages intelligence to remain outside the actual operating model.

### Option B — Fully autonomous AI software development

AI receives a goal and independently plans, decides, implements, verifies, accepts, and operates the resulting system.

#### Advantages

* maximum potential throughput;
* minimal human interaction;
* simple external interface: goal in, result out.

#### Disadvantages

* collapses analytical capability into authority;
* weakens separation of duties;
* allows executor self-approval;
* creates unacceptable ambiguity around risk ownership;
* makes governance dependent on model behavior;
* provides poor handling of irreversible or cross-boundary decisions;
* conflicts with ADR-0006 and existing Monad authority semantics.

### Option C — External AI-driven lifecycle alongside EOS

Adopt an external AI-DLC, AI-SDLC, or similar lifecycle as a parallel workflow authority.

#### Advantages

* faster access to external workflow patterns;
* reduced initial design effort;
* potential compatibility with external AI-development tooling.

#### Disadvantages

* creates competing lifecycle semantics;
* duplicates task, decision, approval, dependency, evidence, and state concepts;
* risks transferring authority to a vendor or framework;
* fragments traceability;
* conflicts directly with ADR-0006.

### Option D — Monad-native AI-driven, human-governed, evidence-closed engineering

AI proactively drives analysis and workflow progression while EOS controls lifecycle and policy, explicit authorities control consequential decisions, bounded executors perform authorized effects, and evidence/review control acceptance.

#### Advantages

* uses AI as an active engineering participant;
* preserves one authority model;
* supports incremental progression from assistance to bounded autonomy;
* enables adaptive workflow depth;
* keeps providers and harnesses replaceable;
* supports stronger traceability and auditability;
* composes with existing Monad semantics;
* allows human attention to focus on consequential judgment rather than routine coordination.

#### Disadvantages

* requires additional normative contracts;
* creates more explicit boundaries between planning, decision, execution, verification, and review;
* requires autonomy and escalation policies;
* requires context compilation and pathway reasoning to become inspectable;
* adds conformance and traceability obligations before higher autonomy can be trusted.

## Decision

Choose **Option D — Monad-native AI-driven, human-governed, evidence-closed engineering**.

AI-driven engineering becomes the preferred operating model where suitable AI capability exists.

This does not mean every engineering operation requires AI.

It means Monad's default architecture SHALL assume that capable AI participants may:

* inspect governed engineering state;
* identify the next meaningful engineering problem;
* compile relevant context;
* produce candidate plans;
* identify unresolved ambiguity;
* ask targeted clarifying questions;
* generate decision options;
* recommend preferred options;
* propose lifecycle transitions;
* create candidate artifacts;
* execute within delegated boundaries;
* run or request verification;
* interpret evidence;
* identify unexpected work;
* propose changes;
* recommend replanning.

Binding authority remains determined by governance.

## AI-driven workflow initiative

AI MAY initiate non-binding engineering activity without first receiving a separate approval for every analytical step.

Examples include:

* inspecting repository state;
* detecting inconsistent requirements;
* identifying missing decisions;
* calculating dependency impact;
* proposing an engineering pathway;
* generating alternatives;
* requesting clarification;
* predicting required verification;
* detecting stale assumptions;
* proposing follow-on work.

Such initiative SHALL remain non-binding until it reaches an action governed by explicit authority, policy, lifecycle, capability, or approval requirements.

The system SHALL therefore distinguish:

```text
may think
may inspect
may recommend
may propose
may execute
may approve
may accept
```

as separate classes of capability and authority.

## Context compilation

AI-driven work SHALL operate from governed context rather than unconstrained transcript accumulation.

Context compilation SHOULD derive the smallest sufficient engineering context from authoritative and relevant sources including, as applicable:

* intent;
* requirements;
* architecture;
* specifications;
* decisions;
* authority;
* policy;
* lifecycle state;
* Work Packet scope;
* dependencies;
* prior evidence;
* unresolved risks;
* acceptance criteria;
* execution environment;
* operational observations.

Compiled context is a projection.

It SHALL NOT become a competing canonical state.

Provider-specific prompts, conversation transcripts, caches, embeddings, and temporary reasoning state SHALL NOT become authoritative merely because they were used during engineering.

## Adaptive engineering pathway

AI SHOULD propose an engineering pathway appropriate to the work rather than mechanically invoking every possible engineering activity.

Pathway depth MAY vary according to:

* consequence;
* reversibility;
* uncertainty;
* novelty;
* architecture impact;
* security impact;
* operational risk;
* number and criticality of dependencies;
* evidence quality;
* unresolved decisions;
* lifecycle state;
* policy;
* delegated autonomy.

A low-risk reversible change may require a lightweight pathway.

A high-risk architectural, security, migration, production, legal, or irreversible change may require deeper planning, explicit decisions, independent review, additional verification, or higher authority.

Adaptive pathways MAY:

* omit optional activities;
* deepen required activities;
* reorder non-mandatory analysis;
* add targeted investigation;
* request decisions;
* increase required review;
* increase evidence requirements.

Adaptive pathways MUST NOT:

* bypass mandatory EOS gates;
* waive required authority;
* silently redefine acceptance criteria;
* eliminate required independent verification;
* ignore unresolved blocking decisions;
* redefine lifecycle state;
* grant new execution capability.

Material pathway variation SHALL be inspectable and explainable.

## Ambiguity and clarification

AI SHALL distinguish ordinary implementation discretion from ambiguity that affects consequential engineering meaning.

Material ambiguity includes ambiguity concerning:

* product intent;
* public behavior;
* requirements;
* system boundaries;
* architecture;
* security posture;
* accepted risk;
* data handling;
* legal or compliance obligations;
* production behavior;
* destructive effects;
* irreversible migration;
* authority;
* acceptance criteria.

Material ambiguity SHALL be surfaced rather than silently resolved through model inference.

Where existing delegated authority is sufficient, the authorized owner MAY resolve the ambiguity.

Where it is not sufficient, the ambiguity SHALL route into native decision, approval, change-control, or escalation semantics.

## Human sovereignty

Human sovereignty is grounded in accountable authority rather than an assumption of permanent human analytical superiority.

Humans remain responsible, according to governance, for matters such as:

* mission;
* product commitments;
* architecture authority;
* accepted risk;
* security exceptions;
* legal posture;
* operational accountability;
* delegation;
* revocation;
* irreversible commitments;
* final escalation.

This ADR does not require a human to manually approve every reversible or low-consequence action.

Authority MAY be delegated to automation when:

* scope is explicit;
* permitted decisions or effects are explicit;
* limits are explicit;
* evidence obligations are explicit;
* delegation is observable;
* delegation is revocable;
* policy permits the delegation;
* the delegating authority possesses the authority being delegated.

Delegated AI authority SHALL never exceed the delegator's own authority.

## Autonomy profiles

AI-assisted, AI-driven, and bounded AI-autonomous engineering SHALL be modeled as **profiles of one operating model**, not separate methodologies.

### AI-assisted profile

Typical characteristics:

* human initiates most actions;
* AI provides analysis and candidate outputs;
* human selects or executes most consequential steps.

### AI-driven profile

Typical characteristics:

* AI proactively proposes and coordinates engineering progression;
* AI identifies clarification and decision needs;
* humans disposition consequential authority;
* AI performs authorized work and verification activities.

This is the preferred default profile.

### Bounded AI-autonomous profile

Typical characteristics:

* AI may independently progress work within an explicit delegated envelope;
* routine decisions within delegated authority may not require synchronous human intervention;
* capability and scope remain bounded;
* evidence and observability remain mandatory;
* escalation triggers remain explicit;
* autonomy may be revoked.

Autonomy level SHALL NOT determine whether AI may reason or recommend.

It determines what binding actions AI may perform without additional approval.

## Progressive autonomy

Higher autonomy SHALL be:

* explicit;
* bounded;
* policy-controlled;
* consequence-aware;
* evidence-backed;
* observable;
* attributable;
* reversible where feasible;
* revocable.

Autonomy SHOULD increase only when evidence demonstrates adequate reliability within the relevant class of work.

Evidence supporting autonomy MAY include:

* validation success;
* unauthorized-action rate;
* review findings;
* rollback rate;
* defect escape rate;
* requirement-conformance rate;
* escalation quality;
* recovery performance;
* reproducibility;
* security findings;
* operational outcomes.

Success in one domain SHALL NOT automatically authorize autonomy in a materially different domain.

## Required invariants

The operating model SHALL preserve the following invariants.

### I01 — Intent traceability

Every governed execution SHALL trace to authorized engineering intent.

### I02 — Initiative without authority

AI MAY initiate analysis, planning, clarification, recommendations, and proposed transitions without acquiring authority merely by doing so.

### I03 — Ambiguity escalation

Material consequential ambiguity SHALL be surfaced rather than silently resolved.

### I04 — Adaptive workflow

Engineering pathway breadth and depth MAY adapt to risk, complexity, lifecycle state, dependencies, evidence, reversibility, and uncertainty.

### I05 — Explainable rigor

Materially skipped, reordered, added, or deepened engineering activities SHALL be explainable.

### I06 — Native decision semantics

Consequential decisions SHALL use native Monad authority, Decision, Approval, ADR, Change Request, Review, and related semantics where applicable.

### I07 — No probabilistic promotion

Probabilistic confidence SHALL NOT promote AI output into canonical or normative engineering truth.

### I08 — Bounded execution

Execution SHALL remain within authorized scope, Work Packet, environment, capability, and autonomy boundaries.

### I09 — Governing drift

Material drift in governing inputs SHALL invalidate, suspend, or require recompilation of stale execution authority.

### I10 — Evidence independence

Executor assertions and self-tests SHALL NOT be sufficient evidence where policy requires independent verification.

### I11 — Review independence

Required review independence SHALL be enforceable according to consequence and policy.

### I12 — Reconstructability

Consequential planning, decisions, approvals, executions, verification, review, changes, and acceptance SHALL remain reconstructably traceable.

### I13 — Transcript non-authority

Raw AI transcripts SHALL NOT be mandatory canonical engineering state.

### I14 — Provider neutrality

AI provider, model, harness, or vendor identity SHALL NOT itself confer semantic authority.

### I15 — Progressive autonomy

Autonomy SHALL remain explicit, evidence-backed, bounded, observable, policy-controlled, and revocable.

### I16 — Evidence feedback

Engineering and operational evidence MAY inform subsequent AI reasoning while retaining its actual authority classification.

## Relationship to EOS

This operating model spans the existing EOS layers.

It does not create another layer.

### EOSB — Bootstrap

AI may help discover repository state, identify missing prerequisites, and construct initial engineering context.

### EOSP — Planning

AI may analyze intent, dependencies, uncertainty, risk, and evidence and propose adaptive engineering pathways, Work Cycles, Work Packets, sequencing, and decision needs.

### EOSE — Execution

AI or other executors may perform work only inside governed authorization and execution boundaries.

### EOSV — Verification

AI may run, interpret, and propose verification activity, but verification authority remains determined by governed validator/evidence semantics.

### EOSR — Review

AI may participate in review subject to independence policy. An executor SHALL NOT satisfy independent-review requirements merely by reviewing its own work through the same insufficiently independent execution identity.

### EOSC — Change control

Unexpected discoveries, changed requirements, architecture changes, or governing drift SHALL route through existing change-control semantics rather than being silently absorbed into execution scope.

### EOSL — Release lifecycle

AI may assemble release readiness information, identify blockers, and recommend progression. Release authority remains governed.

### EOSM — Maintenance

AI may identify maintenance work, propose classification and prioritization, and execute authorized maintenance within the same authority and evidence constraints.

## Relationship to ADR-0006

ADR-0006 remains authoritative.

ADR-0008 applies ADR-0006's selective-assimilation rule to the broader operating model.

External AI-driven development frameworks MAY continue to serve as:

* design donors;
* research inputs;
* compatibility targets;
* migration inputs;
* import/export formats.

They SHALL NOT become a second EOS.

Monad-native semantics remain authoritative.

## Relationship to ADR-0007

ADR-0007 is currently Proposed.

If accepted, it defines a Governed Execution Harness as the stable effect and execution boundary between EOS and replaceable executors.

ADR-0008 defines a broader concern:

> how AI participates throughout governed engineering.

ADR-0007 defines a narrower concern:

> how authorized execution crosses the boundary into an executor or harness.

The intended composition is:

```text
ADR-0008
AI-driven engineering operating model
        ↓
EOS planning / authority / decision / policy
        ↓
ADR-0007 governed execution boundary
        ↓
replaceable executor / agent harness
        ↓
evidence
        ↓
EOS verification / review / change control
```

ADR-0008 SHALL NOT make ADR-0007 Accepted implicitly.

If ADR-0007 is rejected or materially redesigned, ADR-0008 remains valid provided an alternative execution boundary preserves the invariants established here.

## Canonical model constraints

The AI-driven operating model MUST NOT introduce a parallel AI state model.

`.eos/state/current.json` remains the sole current EOS operational authority.

Existing concepts SHALL be reused where sufficient, including:

* `PI`;
* `WC`;
* `WP`;
* `CR`;
* `DEC`;
* `APR`;
* `DEP`;
* `REV`;
* `EVID`;
* `TRC`.

The presence of AI SHALL NOT justify introducing duplicate canonical concepts such as:

```text
AI-PLAN
AI-TASK
AI-DECISION
AI-APPROVAL
AI-EVIDENCE
AI-WORKFLOW
AI-CONTEXT
AI-STATE
```

unless a separately governed semantic-gap analysis demonstrates that existing concepts are insufficient.

## Provider and harness neutrality

Provider and harness choice is an execution concern, not a source of engineering authority.

Monad SHOULD support multiple AI providers and harnesses where beneficial.

Provider selection MAY depend on:

* capability;
* reliability;
* latency;
* cost;
* privacy;
* data residency;
* tool support;
* context limits;
* model specialization;
* evaluation evidence.

Provider selection SHALL NOT alter the governing semantics of:

* requirements;
* authority;
* decisions;
* Work Packets;
* approvals;
* evidence;
* verification;
* review;
* acceptance.

Provider failure SHALL NOT corrupt canonical engineering state.

## Evidence and acceptance

AI-generated claims are candidate claims unless elevated through the appropriate governed process.

Evidence SHALL retain:

* provenance;
* target;
* validator or source;
* result;
* applicable scope;
* freshness;
* authority classification where relevant.

Evidence MAY be probabilistic.

Acceptance authority SHALL remain governed.

A high-confidence AI statement SHALL NOT substitute for required deterministic verification.

A large quantity of weak evidence SHALL NOT automatically become strong evidence.

## Review independence

Where independent review is required, independence SHALL be evaluated as a governed property rather than inferred from the existence of a second model invocation.

Potential independence dimensions include:

* executor identity;
* harness identity;
* model/provider identity;
* context isolation;
* prompt independence;
* organizational role;
* human reviewer involvement.

Exact independence predicates are deferred to specification and policy.

## Security, privacy, and operations

AI-driven engineering increases the importance of explicit trust boundaries because generated reasoning may result in consequential effects.

The operating model SHALL preserve:

* least privilege;
* deny-by-default capability behavior where authority is unresolved;
* bounded filesystem/process/network access;
* explicit external-service permissions;
* sensitive-context minimization;
* secret protection;
* stale-context detection;
* observable effect requests;
* cancellation;
* timeout;
* retry limits;
* escalation;
* recovery;
* incident reconstruction.

Raw model reasoning or private chain-of-thought SHALL NOT be required as audit evidence.

Auditability SHALL instead rely on governed inputs and outputs such as:

* intent;
* plan;
* material questions;
* decisions;
* approvals;
* execution identity;
* requested and performed effects;
* evidence;
* verification;
* review;
* resulting changes;
* acceptance.

No new production or repository privilege is authorized merely by accepting this ADR.

## Rollback

Before acceptance, this proposal may be revised or rejected normally.

After acceptance, rollback of the decision meaning requires a new ADR that supersedes ADR-0008.

Implementation introduced under this decision SHALL remain incrementally reversible wherever practical.

Rollback of implementation SHALL preserve:

* canonical state;
* decisions;
* historical evidence;
* provenance;
* recorded approvals;
* prior accepted ADR meaning.

No implementation tranche may rely on an irreversible migration without separately documenting the migration, recovery, and authority implications.

## Required conformance scenarios

The normative tranche SHALL define and trace at least the following scenarios.

### Scenario A — Low-risk reversible change

A small reversible change receives a lightweight engineering pathway without unnecessary ceremony while preserving mandatory EOS gates.

### Scenario B — Ambiguous product intent

AI encounters materially ambiguous product behavior and requests a governed decision rather than silently guessing.

### Scenario C — Architecture change during execution

Execution discovers that the authorized work requires a material architecture change. Work routes through EOSC rather than silently broadening scope.

### Scenario D — Bounded autonomous Work Packet

An explicitly authorized low-risk Work Packet progresses autonomously within its delegated capability envelope and evidence requirements.

### Scenario E — Security-sensitive work

A security-sensitive change causes the adaptive pathway to increase required authority, verification, and independent review.

### Scenario F — Independent review

Policy requires independent review. The executor cannot satisfy the requirement with trivial self-review.

### Scenario G — Governing-input drift

A requirement, specification, decision, or policy governing an active execution materially changes. The stale execution authority is invalidated or suspended.

### Scenario H — Provider failure

An AI provider becomes unavailable or fails mid-work. Canonical engineering state remains valid and another conforming executor may resume from governed state.

### Scenario I — Human denial

An accountable authority rejects a proposed consequential decision. AI replans according to the denial rather than repeatedly attempting to obtain the same rejected outcome.

### Scenario J — Evidence invalidates assumption

Verification or operational evidence invalidates an assumption used during planning. The result routes into replanning or change control rather than being ignored.

## Validation

ADR-0008 is validated when the normative tranche demonstrates that:

* ADR-0006 remains intact and authoritative;
* no second lifecycle or canonical current-state authority has been introduced;
* AI initiative and binding authority are explicitly distinct;
* AI-assisted, AI-driven, and bounded autonomous modes operate as profiles of one model;
* adaptive workflow depth preserves mandatory EOS gates;
* material ambiguity routes to native authority and decision semantics;
* every governed execution traces to authorized intent;
* governing drift invalidates stale execution authority;
* review independence can be expressed without provider lock-in;
* acceptance remains evidence-based;
* raw AI transcripts are not required as canonical state;
* provider failure does not invalidate canonical engineering state;
* autonomy can be explicitly revoked;
* all required conformance scenarios have normative owners;
* the active MVP Work Packet scope remains unchanged;
* machine implementation remains separately authorized.

## Reconsideration triggers

Reconsider this decision if evidence demonstrates that:

* AI-driven workflow initiative consistently reduces engineering quality or increases consequential error beyond acceptable limits;
* adaptive pathways cannot remain deterministic enough to govern safely;
* required human decision load becomes materially higher than the prior operating model;
* provider-neutral execution proves impractical without unacceptable loss of capability;
* the distinction between AI initiative and authority cannot be mechanically or operationally maintained;
* a superior operating model provides equivalent accountability and evidence with materially lower complexity.

A reconsideration SHALL occur through a new ADR rather than rewriting the accepted meaning of ADR-0008.

## Consequences

### Positive

* AI becomes an explicit first-class engineering participant rather than an informal tool.
* Humans can focus attention on consequential authority instead of routine orchestration.
* AI planning, clarification, execution, verification, and learning operate under one coherent governance model.
* The architecture supports progression from assistance to bounded autonomy without replacing EOS.
* Providers and harnesses remain replaceable.
* Adaptive rigor can reduce ceremony for simple work while increasing rigor for high-risk work.
* Acceptance remains grounded in evidence rather than model confidence.
* The model provides a coherent foundation for later multi-agent and autonomous engineering capabilities.

### Negative

* More explicit contracts are required across planning, authority, execution, verification, and review.
* Progressive autonomy requires evidence collection and policy design.
* Adaptive pathways introduce additional reasoning and observability requirements.
* Provider-neutrality requires abstraction and conformance effort.
* Some engineering work will stop more often at explicit ambiguity boundaries instead of allowing convenient assumptions.

### Neutral or follow-on

* ADR-0007 may provide the execution-harness realization of this model if separately accepted.
* EOS-AI-0001 will define the cross-layer operational contract.
* Existing product requirements require refinement rather than replacement.
* New AIENG specifications will own detailed behavior.
* Product-runtime implementation remains separately planned and authorized.
